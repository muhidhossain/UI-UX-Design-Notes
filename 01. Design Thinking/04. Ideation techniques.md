# Design Thinking — Ideation Techniques

_Last updated: 2026-08-25_

## Overview

Divergent thinking is the principle — defer judgment, maximize quantity. These are the concrete techniques that make it actually happen in a room instead of staying an abstract rule nobody follows: brainwriting, Crazy 8s, SCAMPER, and worst possible idea.

## Why plain brainstorming underperforms

Unstructured "everyone shout out ideas" brainstorming has well-documented failure modes:

- **Production blocking** — only one person can talk at a time, so while someone else is speaking, others' ideas go stale or get forgotten.
- **Evaluation apprehension** — even with a "no judgment" rule stated out loud, people self-censor ideas that might sound dumb, especially with senior people in the room.
- **Groupthink / anchoring** — the first few ideas said out loud shape everyone else's thinking, narrowing the range instead of widening it.

The techniques below exist specifically to route around these three problems.

## Brainwriting (silent generation)

Each person writes ideas individually and silently first — on sticky notes or paper — before anything is shared aloud. Only after that individual pass does the group share and build on what's there.

- Fixes production blocking (everyone's "talking" — i.e. writing — at once).
- Fixes evaluation apprehension (no live audience judging the idea as it's spoken).
- Fixes anchoring (nobody's first idea is influenced by hearing someone else's first).

Usually the strongest default starting technique precisely because it neutralizes all three brainstorming failure modes at once.

## Crazy 8s

A tightly timed sketching exercise: fold a sheet of paper into 8 panels, then sketch 8 distinct ideas in 8 minutes — one idea per panel, roughly a minute each.

- The time pressure is the mechanism: a minute isn't enough to overthink or polish, which forces past the first 2–3 obvious ideas into less obvious territory.
- Sketches, not descriptions — visual thinking surfaces different ideas than writing does, and keeps the format low-fidelity so nobody's "finishing" an idea prematurely.
- Works well paired with brainwriting: silent individual generation, but structured and time-boxed.

## SCAMPER

A mnemonic checklist of transformations, applied to something that already exists (a product, feature, or process) to force new angles instead of starting from a blank page:

| Letter | Prompt | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ubstitute | Swap a component for something else | Replace a form field with a chat interface |
| **C**ombine | Merge with another feature/idea | Combine search and filtering into one input |
| **A**dapt | Borrow a solution from a different context | Adapt a video game's tutorial pattern for onboarding |
| **M**odify (Magnify/Minify) | Exaggerate or shrink an attribute | Make the confirmation step much heavier, or remove it entirely |
| **P**ut to another use | Repurpose for a different audience/use case | Use an internal admin tool as a customer-facing feature |
| **E**liminate | Remove a component entirely | Remove the login requirement |
| **R**everse | Invert the order or logic | Let the user pick the outcome first, then the inputs |

Unlike brainwriting/Crazy 8s (blank-page generation), SCAMPER is best when there's already a concrete artifact to iterate on — it's a lens for breaking fixation on the current design, not a way to generate from nothing.

## Worst possible idea

Deliberately generate the *worst* ideas the group can think of first, then invert or extract the useful kernel from each.

- Useful specifically when a group is stuck or over-anchored on "reasonable" ideas — permission to be bad removes the self-censorship that blocks divergent thinking.
- Often surfaces a real insight hiding inside a joke: "the worst onboarding would email you a 40-page PDF" → inverts into "what's the minimum a new user actually needs on day one?"

## Picking a technique

- Starting from a blank problem, no existing artifact → brainwriting, then Crazy 8s to push past the obvious.
- Iterating on something that already exists → SCAMPER.
- Group is stuck, anchored, or too polite/serious → worst possible idea.

## Facilitation practices that apply across all of them

- Time-box everything visibly — a countdown timer, not just an intention, is what actually enforces the "don't overthink it" pressure.
- Individual-first, group-second — silent generation before any sharing, every time.
- Keep the "no judgment" rule enforced by the facilitator in the moment, not just stated at the start — actively interrupt evaluative comments during the divergent window.

## Key Takeaways

- Plain unstructured brainstorming underperforms due to production blocking, evaluation apprehension, and anchoring — structured techniques exist to route around each.
- Brainwriting (silent, individual-first generation) is the strongest default starting technique.
- Crazy 8s uses time pressure to force sketching past the obvious first few ideas.
- SCAMPER is for iterating on an existing artifact, not blank-page generation — pick the technique based on whether there's already something to iterate on.
- Worst possible idea breaks fixation for stuck or over-polite groups by removing the pressure to sound reasonable.
